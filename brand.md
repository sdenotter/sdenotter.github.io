## 01 — BRAND NAME

**Primary: Scott Denotter**

Your name is your brand. It is distinctive, memorable, and professional. It builds a personal reputation that compounds over time — clients refer 'Scott', not a faceless agency. This is the right choice for your primary audience of SMBs who buy based on trust in a person, not a logo.



---

## 02 — TAGLINE

### Primary Tagline

> # Fix the process. Not just the code.

This tagline does three things simultaneously: it signals your preference for simplicity over complexity, it speaks directly to clients who feel overwhelmed by their own processes, and it subtly positions you against developers who over-engineer. It is both a promise and a philosophy.

### Alternative Taglines

| Tagline | When to use |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fix the process, not just the code.** | When speaking to operations-heavy clients with broken workflows. |
| **You describe the problem. I find the fix.** | For direct-response contexts: ads, cold outreach, referral landing pages. |
| **Built right. Not built complicated.** | When competing against over-engineered agency proposals. |

## 03 — POSITIONING

### Positioning Statement

For small and mid-size businesses that run on broken or manual processes but don't have a dedicated developer, **Scott Denotter** diagnoses the real operational problem before writing a single line of code — then builds the simplest, most effective solution. Unlike generalist freelancers who build what you ask for, **Scott tells you what you actually need.**

### Target Audience

*   **Primary:** Owners and operations managers at small-to-mid-size businesses (5–100 employees) without a full-time developer or IT person on their team. They run on a mix of spreadsheets, manual processes, and off-the-shelf tools that no longer fit. 
*   **Secondary:** Technical leads at larger companies looking for training on AI-assisted development practices. This audience lends credibility to the primary positioning without diluting it.

### Competitive Differentiation

*   Most freelancers are incentivised to build the complicated thing. Scott is not.
*   Most developers need a clear spec to start. Scott doesn't — he creates the spec.
*   Most generalists hide being a generalist. Scott reframes it as a diagnostic advantage.

## 04 — BRAND PILLARS

These four pillars are the non-negotiable truths of the Scott Denotter brand. 

| Pillar | Philosophy |
| :--- | :--- |
| **01 DIAGNOSIS FIRST** | **Understand before you build.** Scott's first question is never "what do you want built?" It's "what's actually going wrong?" The diagnostic process is the product, not a free add-on. |
| **02 SIMPLICITY AS CRAFT** | **The simplest working solution is the best solution.** It takes more skill to resist over-engineering than to indulge it. Scott regularly talks clients down from complexity they don't need. |
| **03 OUTCOME FIRST** | **Clients don't buy code. They buy results.** Every project is framed around what changed for the business — time saved, errors eliminated, decisions made faster. |
| **04 HONEST EXPERTISE** | **Be the expert who tells the truth.** Credibility comes from candour, not credentials. Scott understands the craft deeply enough to teach it, which provides proof of expertise. |

## 05 — VOICE & TONE

### Brand Voice

The Scott Denotter voice is the voice of a trusted senior colleague. It is warm but not casual, confident but not boastful, direct but never blunt.

| Dimension | Always | Never |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Confident** | Leads with the outcome. States recommendations directly. | Hedges with "I think maybe..." or over-qualifies every claim. |
| **Plain-spoken** | Uses the simplest word that is accurate. Avoids jargon. | Uses technical terminology to signal expertise. Writes like a whitepaper. |
| **Empathetic** | Acknowledges the client's frustration before describing the fix. | Dismisses complexity as obvious or the client as unsophisticated. |
| **Honest** | Names tradeoffs. Explains what a solution won't do. | Oversells. Promises outcomes outside his control. |
| **Dry wit** | Uses wry observations that make a client feel understood. | Makes jokes at the client's expense or uses humor to avoid a hard truth. |

## 06 — CORE MESSAGE

### The Single Most Important Thing

> **You don't need a developer who builds what you ask for.**
> 
> _You need one who figures out what you actually need — then builds the simplest thing that solves it._

### Message Hierarchy

1.  I understand your problem — probably better than you've been able to articulate it.
2.  I find the highest-leverage fix, not the most impressive one.
3.  Simple solutions are harder to build than complicated ones. I choose them anyway.
4.  I have enough expertise to teach this to other developers — which means I won't be learning on your time.

### What This Brand Is Not

*   **Not a feature list.** Never lead with technologies or languages.
*   **Not modest.** Underselling is as damaging as overselling.
*   **Not a generalist hiding behind a stack.** The breadth is the point.
*   **Not an agency pretending to be a person.** The personal brand is the asset.
